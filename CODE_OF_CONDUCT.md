# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to help. Criticise the code, not
the person who wrote it.

## What is expected

- **Argue about the work.** Strong technical disagreement is welcome and useful.
  "This breaks the contradiction rule and here is the case" is a good comment.
  "This is amateur work" is not, and it is also less informative.
- **Assume good faith.** Most bad pull requests are someone who did not know a
  rule, not someone being careless.
- **Accept a decision once it is made.** Disagreement is fine; relitigating the
  same point in every thread is not.
- **Remember that most people here are not paid to be.** Including the
  maintainer, and including whoever filed the issue you find annoying.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, sustained hostility, or personal attacks.
- Demeaning comments about someone's identity, including but not limited to
  gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, disability,
  physical appearance, body size, race, ethnicity, age, religion, nationality,
  or level of experience.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately derailing threads, or persistent unconstructive negativity.

## Scope

This applies in every project space: issues, pull requests, discussions,
commit messages, code comments, and when representing the project elsewhere.

Behaviour outside these spaces is generally not the project's business, with one
exception: conduct that makes someone reasonably unsafe participating *here* is
in scope wherever it happened.

## Enforcement

Email **security@omem-cloud.com** and put "conduct" in the subject, or
[open a private security advisory](https://github.com/troybrandonc-bit/Omem/security/advisories/new)
and say at the top that it is a conduct report.

That address is shared with security reporting rather than being a dedicated
conduct@ mailbox, which is worth stating plainly: on a project with one
maintainer a separate address would be the same inbox with a different label. If
that changes, this section changes with it.

Reports are read by the maintainer and kept confidential. You will get an
acknowledgement within 3 business days.

Responses, roughly in order:

1. **A private word**: the usual outcome, and usually the end of it.
2. **A public correction** in the thread, where the behaviour was public and
   left a misleading impression.
3. **A temporary block** from project spaces.
4. **A permanent ban**, for severe or repeated behaviour.

Two things worth saying plainly, because they are where codes of conduct usually
go wrong:

**The maintainer is not exempt.** If the problem is the maintainer's behaviour
and a private report is not something you are willing to send, say so publicly.
A project where the person with commit access is above the rules does not have
rules.

**Reporting is not punished.** Retaliation against someone who reports, in good
faith, is itself a violation and is treated as a serious one. A report that
turns out to be mistaken is not a violation of anything.
